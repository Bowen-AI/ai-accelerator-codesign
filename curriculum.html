Course syllabus

A six-week pathway from workload analysis to architectural design.

Approximately 48–60 hours of self-directed study, technical investigation and writing. Each module builds toward a final architecture review.

6 modules8–10 hours each
Python 3.10+core lab environment
Open resourcesMIT and Stanford

Learning outcomes

Quantitative analysis

Calculate operation counts, data movement and limiting roofline performance for matrix workloads.

Architectural reasoning

Explain compute-memory trade-offs, locality, mapping and the implications of hardware implementation constraints.

Technical communication

Present reproducible evidence for a hardware change and articulate the uncertainties that require further validation.

Indicative weekly cadence: lectures and notes (2 h), derivation and analysis (2 h), hands-on lab (3–4 h), design write-up (1–2 h).

MODULE 01 / 06

Workload characterization

Develop a resource accounting model for representative Transformer operators.

Concepts

  • Matrix multiplication as M×N×K; operation counts and precision
  • Transformers: attention, MLP, prefill and autoregressive decoding
  • Arithmetic intensity; roofline compute and bandwidth limits

Primary references

  • Stanford CS336 — resource accounting and inference ↗
  • MIT 6.5930 — DNN workloads and Transformers ↗
Lab and analysis
Execute the roofline demonstration. Compare batch-one decoding with a large prefill projection. Calculate the modeled data traffic and explain why their bottlenecks differ.
Assessment
Workload analysis brief containing two GEMM calculations, a roofline estimate and the modeling assumptions.
MODULE 02 / 06

Accelerator architecture and dataflow

Relate compute structures and data movement to effective accelerator performance.

Concepts

  • Processing-element arrays, systolic execution and dataflow
  • SRAM, HBM, tiling and operational reuse
  • Peak compute versus sustained throughput; bandwidth ceilings

Primary references

  • MIT 6.5930 — mapping and dataflow ↗
  • Stanford CS217 — accelerator architecture ↗
  • Timeloop/Accelergy project ↗
Lab and analysis
Explore compute throughput and bandwidth increases independently using the supplied sweep. Sketch one tiling strategy that reduces external transfers.
Assessment
A design comparison with at least four architecture scenarios and a dataflow diagram.
MODULE 03 / 06

Model-aware hardware optimization

Evaluate algorithmic choices in terms of hardware demand, not just parameter or FLOP counts.

Concepts

  • Attention and KV-cache characteristics
  • Quantization, mixed precision, sparsity and pruning
  • Batch size, sequence length and arithmetic utilization

Primary references

  • MIT 6.5940 — efficient AI methods ↗
  • Stanford CS336 — hardware and inference lectures ↗
Lab and analysis
Compare FP16 and assumed INT8 data footprints. Identify which accuracy, kernel-support, utilization and memory-transfer assumptions are required to translate a smaller model into faster inference.
Assessment
An optimization assessment documenting benefits, caveats and one case where a theoretical gain may not improve throughput.
MODULE 04 / 06

RTL, synthesis and physical constraints

Connect conceptual architecture changes to concrete digital circuit implementation.

Concepts

  • Architecture, microarchitecture, RTL and cycle-level behavior
  • Registers, pipelining, timing constraints and synthesis
  • Placement/routing, critical paths, area, power and timing closure

Primary references

  • Stanford CS217 — HLS and remote FPGA projects ↗
  • Verilator documentation ↗
  • OpenROAD documentation ↗
Lab and analysis
Create a register-to-register datapath diagram for a multiply-accumulate unit. Identify two risks that could undermine an attractive architecture estimate. Optional: simulate RTL locally.
Assessment
A block diagram and an implementation-risk note, distinguishing logical correctness from physical timing.
MODULE 05 / 06

Design-space exploration and sensitivity

Compare alternatives under explicit performance assumptions and understand when conclusions reverse.

Concepts

  • Performance versus bandwidth and compute capacity
  • Utilization, traffic multipliers and workload-mix sensitivity
  • Pareto reasoning and shortcomings of simplified analytical models

Primary references

  • MIT 6.5930 — mapping, fusion and design project ↗
  • Timeloop/Accelergy ↗
Lab and analysis
Run the sixteen-scenario architectural sweep; then vary utilization and traffic assumptions. Identify changes that materially alter the design ranking.
Assessment
An experiment report with at least one sensitivity figure and a defensible design shortlist.
MODULE 06 / 06

Capstone architecture review

Formulate a workload-driven hardware proposal, supported by reproducible evidence and a validation strategy.

Concepts

  • Define system objective and workload characteristics
  • Build baseline, experimental alternatives and uncertainty analysis
  • Propose a next-stage hardware validation plan

Primary references

  • MIT 6.5930 — architecture design project ↗
  • Stanford CS217 — implementation perspective ↗
Lab and analysis
Integrate the workload model, architectural alternatives and trade-off experiments into a single technical decision.
Assessment
A five-slide review and short design memo. See the formal capstone brief.

Assessment approach

Progress is demonstrated through quantitative analysis, documented assumptions and reproducible artifacts. No grades or university credit are issued. Technical conclusions should distinguish measured results from analytical projections.
