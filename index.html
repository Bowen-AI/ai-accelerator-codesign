Important hardware clarification. MIT 6.5930 includes accelerator modeling/design labs; Stanford CS217 also includes remote real cloud FPGA evaluation, and Stanford CS336's full assignments may require remote GPU compute. This study plan adapts relevant ideas into an original, software-only core. Official assignments and course resources may have access restrictions.

What you'll learn

Not another generic introduction to AI. You'll learn to connect workload characteristics to architectural decisions and the limits imposed by implementation.

01 / Diagnose

Workload behavior

Understand GEMM, Transformer prefill/decode, KV caches, arithmetic intensity and bottleneck transitions.

02 / Design

Architecture choices

Explore compute arrays, data reuse, SRAM/HBM, precision, tiling, bandwidth and interconnect.

03 / Defend

Technical recommendations

Explain why a proposed improvement helps, quantify sensitivity, and identify what needs real-hardware validation.

The 6-week syllabus

Each week: public materials (~2 h), analysis (~2 h), lab (~3–4 h), design memo (~1–2 h).

0 of 6 weeks complete
WEEK 01Workloads

Where does time go?

Prefill vs decode; GEMM; ops, DRAM bytes, arithmetic intensity, roofline.

  • Study MIT 6.5930 DNN and Transformers notes.
  • Use selected Stanford CS336 modeling lectures.
  • Deliver: compute vs memory-bound benchmark worksheet.
MIT notes ↗Stanford CS336 ↗
WEEK 02Architecture

Compute, memory & dataflow

MAC arrays, systolic ideas, SRAM/HBM, spatial and temporal mappings, data reuse.

  • MIT mapping/dataflow notes; CS217 architecture coverage.
  • Learn the Timeloop/Accelergy modeling workflow.
  • Deliver: 2×2 compute/bandwidth design sweep.
Stanford CS217 ↗Timeloop ↗
WEEK 03Co-design

Change the model, change the chip

KV cache, batch size, pruning, quantization, activation size and utilization.

  • MIT 6.5940 pruning/quantization lectures.
  • CS336 selected systems and attention discussions.
  • Deliver: FP16 vs INT8 assumptions report.
MIT 6.5940 ↗Stanford CS336 ↗
WEEK 04Implementation

Architecture → RTL → silicon

Microarchitecture, pipeline, registers, logic synthesis, placement/routing, PPA and timing.

  • CS217 HLS / cloud-FPGA scope; Verilator and OpenROAD.
  • Model a register-transfer pipeline; no physical hardware.
  • Deliver: block diagram + timing/area risk review.
Verilator ↗OpenROAD ↗
WEEK 05Evaluation

Design-space exploration

Bottleneck shifts, mapping, fusion, Pareto frontiers, sensitivity and uncertainty.

  • MIT 6.5930 architecture mapping/fusion materials.
  • Read AccelForge modeling assumptions.
  • Deliver: compare multiple architectural options.
AccelForge ↗MIT labs ↗
WEEK 06Capstone

Make the recommendation

Translate an AI workload bottleneck into an architecture change with measurable evidence.

  • Baseline → experiments → risks → validation plan.
  • Distinguish analytical projections from silicon measurements.
  • Deliver: 5-slide design review and technical memo.

Actual university material — linked, not mirrored

MIT 6.5930 ↗

Hardware Architecture for Deep Learning. Primary foundation: mapping, sparsity, precision, architecture modeling, design project. Public 2026 notes, no 2026 lecture recordings.

Stanford CS217 ↗

Hardware Accelerators for Machine Learning. Architecture plus HLS and real cloud FPGA evaluation in the enrolled course. Some materials or tools may not be publicly available.

MIT 6.5940 ↗

TinyML and Efficient AI Computing. Efficient inference, quantization, pruning and deployment. Use publicly posted lecture videos and slides.

Stanford CS336 ↗

Language Modeling from Scratch. Public recordings and assignments. Selected lectures for workload literacy; full systems assignments may require cloud GPUs.
